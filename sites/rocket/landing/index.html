READING 1 · THE BASIC SCIENCE

Two Forces, One Rocket

A rocket engine pushes hot gas downward. The gas pushes the rocket upward with a force called thrust. At the same time, Earth’s gravity pulls the rocket down. To lift off, the upward thrust must be greater than the downward pull of gravity.

After the engine stops, the rocket does not stop at once. It still has momentum, so it keeps moving. Gravity slowly changes its velocity. During a landing, the engine must fire again to reduce the rocket’s downward speed. A safe landing is not only about being slow. The rocket must also be upright and above the landing zone.

Think: Why does the rocket keep falling faster when its engine is off?

READING 2 · BLUE ORIGIN

Slow It, Steer It, Land It

Blue Origin’s New Shepard is a reusable suborbital rocket system. After the capsule separates, the booster falls back toward Earth. Different parts solve different problems.

Ring fins help keep the booster stable. Drag brakes use the air to cut its speed. Aft fins steer it toward the landing pad. Near the ground, the BE-3PM engine restarts and reduces the booster’s speed to about 6 miles per hour. Finally, the landing gear opens for touchdown.

This is an important engineering idea: do not ask one part to solve every problem. Use one system to slow down, another to steer, and another to make the final landing gentle.

Find: Which parts control speed? Which parts control direction?

READING 3 · SPACEX FALCON 9

Why Recovery Is Difficult

Falcon 9 is a two-stage rocket. After separation, the second stage continues toward orbit while the first-stage booster returns. The booster may perform a boostback burn, a re-entry burn and a landing burn. Grid fins guide it through the atmosphere, and landing legs open near touchdown.

The timing must be precise. If the engine restarts too early, the booster may waste propellant. If it restarts too late, there may not be enough time to slow down. Wind can push the booster away from its target. Its mass also changes as fuel is burned, so the same engine thrust can produce a different acceleration.

Engineers therefore test, measure and improve the system over many flights. Recovery is a control problem, not simply a falling problem.

Explain: Why is “start the engine at the right time” not a complete landing plan?
