01 / Earth and Moon are a system

对应目标：观察月球运动，区分“模型图形大小”和真实距离。

The Moon moves around Earth. It takes about 27 days to go around once. The average distance between them is about 384,400 kilometres. That is much farther than most drawings show! In our map, Earth and Moon look large so we can see them. Their sizes are not drawn to scale. Watch the Moon for one simulation day. It does not stay in one place. A spacecraft that takes days to travel must meet the Moon where it will be, not where it is now.

Before you fly · 读后问题

  1. How long does the Moon take to go around Earth once?
  2. Why are Earth and Moon drawn larger than their true scale in the map?
  3. Pause the model. Point to where you think the Moon will be after one day. Then test your prediction.

02 / A burn changes the path

对应目标：从高度 60,000 km 的高地球轨道手动点火，比较点火前后轨迹。

Our spacecraft starts in a high orbit around Earth. It can move without using its engine all the time. Gravity bends its path. To travel toward the Moon, the pilot fires the engine for a short time. A forward burn adds speed and stretches the path farther from Earth. The pilot must choose a direction and a time. After the burn, the spacecraft can coast. It still moves because it already has velocity, and gravity keeps changing its direction. A small mistake early in the trip can make the spacecraft miss the moving Moon.

Before you fly · 读后问题

  1. What does a forward burn do to the spacecraft's speed?
  2. Why does the spacecraft keep moving while the engine is off?
  3. Try two flights with different burn times. Which one comes closer to the Moon? Record evidence.

03 / A flyby is not an orbit

对应目标：观察近月相对速度，尝试减速形成安全绕月轨道。

A spacecraft can pass close to the Moon and still fly away. This is a flyby, not a Moon orbit. To stay near the Moon, the spacecraft often needs to slow down at the right time. The pilot points the engine against the spacecraft's motion relative to the Moon and makes a retrograde burn. The Moon's gravity can then bend the new path around it. But the path must not go through the Moon! Engineers check the lowest and highest points of the orbit. They also save enough fuel for corrections.

Before you fly · 读后问题

  1. What is the difference between a flyby and a Moon orbit?
  2. What does “retrograde” mean in this reading?
  3. Which two numbers in the lab help you decide whether an orbit is safe?
